Dhaka is the capital and largest city of Bangladesh. It sits on the banks of the Buriganga River in the middle of the Ganges delta, and it is the country's political, commercial and cultural centre. It is a busy, crowded and energetic city, known for its rickshaws, its river life, its food and its mix of old and new.

## History

Dhaka became a major regional city in 1610, when it was made the capital of Mughal Bengal and named Jahangirnagar. Later it was the seat of the Nawabs of Dhaka, and buildings from that era, such as Ahsan Manzil, still stand along the river.

In 1905 Dhaka became the capital of the new province of East Bengal and Assam, a change reversed in 1911. After 1947 it was the capital of East Pakistan, and it became the capital of independent Bangladesh after the Liberation War of 1971.

## Geography

Dhaka lies on low, flat land next to a network of rivers. The Buriganga on the southwest edge of the old city has been its main trade route for centuries, and Sadarghat river terminal is still one of the busiest in the country. The city is one of the most densely populated in the world.

## Climate

Dhaka has a tropical monsoon climate with three broad seasons:

- **Cool and dry (roughly November to February):** the most comfortable time to visit, with mild days and cooler evenings.
- **Hot and humid (roughly March to May):** temperatures and humidity rise, with occasional thunderstorms.
- **Monsoon (roughly June to October):** heavy rain is common, and streets in some areas can flood.

## People and language

The people of Dhaka speak Bangla (Bengali), and many younger and professional residents also speak English. Most people are Muslim, and there are also Hindu, Buddhist and Christian communities, which is reflected in the city's mosques, temples and churches.
